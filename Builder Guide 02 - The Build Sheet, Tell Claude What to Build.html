01
FILL THE BUILD SHEET
One page, seven fields
FieldWhat to write
My first systemName and slice sentence, from the First Build Card.
What starts itThe exact event: an order is confirmed, a bill crosses its due date, an enquiry arrives.
What it does or says, word for wordThe message, document or entry, written out fully, in the language your customers and team actually use. If it sends in two languages, write both versions.
What happens on silence or postponementIf the person does not respond, then what, after how many days? If they say “next week”, what date comes back?
When a human steps inThe exact point it escalates, and to whom by name. Plus everything from your blueprint’s sign-off list: prices, credit, discounts, commitments: always a person.
What it must never doCopied from your blueprint’s §4: the untouchable data, the never-without-sign-off list, the error protocol.
Done whenFrom the First Build Card. One state you can observe on your phone.

Two sources feed this page and nothing else: your First Build Card and your blueprint’s §4. If a line is not on either, ask yourself whether it is real or imagined scope.

THE WORD-FOR-WORD RULE: the third field is where most builds go wrong. “Send a polite reminder” is not a rule; the actual sentence, in the actual language, with the actual amount and date in it, is. You know how your customers speak; the machine does not. Write the message you would send with your own thumb, and the system will only ever say what you wrote.
02
THE BUILD PROMPT
Paste to Claude Code

Save the Build Sheet in your project folder, then paste:

Paste to Claude Code

Read my AI Systems Blueprint and my Build Sheet, both in this folder, and the house rules in CLAUDE.md. I want to build exactly what the Build Sheet describes: no more, no less.

First, make a plan: break the build into small phases, where every phase ends with something I can see and test on my phone, and the first phase is always “load my real data and show me one true list.” Tell me for each phase what I will be able to do when it is done. Do not write any code until I approve the plan.

While building, obey the Build Sheet’s “never” list and the house rules at all times. Anything the system is not sure about must be marked “needs review” and shown to a person, never guessed. If any part of my Build Sheet is unclear or contradicts the blueprint, stop and ask me instead of choosing for me.

Read the plan. Ask for simple words on any line you do not understand. Only then say go.

03
BUILDING, SITTING BY SITTING
One phase per sitting

Build one phase per sitting, with the five habits from Builder Guide 00: plan first, one finished thing, commit and push, paste the error when it breaks, close the sitting with a summary note. After each phase:

  1. Commit and push. The live app updates itself.
  2. Open the live app on your phone, not on the computer it was built on.
  3. Test like a businessman: find one customer or order you know cold and check every figure against reality. A screen that is beautiful and wrong is worse than an ugly one that is true.
  4. Log what surprised you in your “rules I learned” note. Every surprise is either a fix for Claude or a line missing from the Build Sheet; update the sheet the same day, so the document and the build never drift apart.
WHEN THE PLAN GOES WRONG MID-BUILD: do not push through a sitting where nothing works. Say: “Stop. Summarise what is broken in simple words, and give me two options: fix forward or go back to the last commit.” Going back costs one sitting; pushing a broken build forward costs a week. Both options are one sentence away, because you committed at every phase.
04
MONTH ONE, WEEK BY WEEK
You are done when
WeekYou are done when
W1 · The list that is trueReal data loaded; one screen shows the live truth of your system; you did the job once by hand through it.
W2 · It drafts, you actIt prepares every message or document correctly, in the right language; a human presses every button; five real cases went through without you composing anything.
W3 · The memory pieceThe part paper cannot do (the promise that reschedules itself, the stage that notifies the next person, the price it remembers) works, and nothing is held in anyone’s head.
W4 · Ready to proveThe “needs review” path and the diary work; the deferred list is untouched; you move to Builder Guide 04 to test and turn on.
THE GATE: you are ready for Builder Guide 04 when all four week rows are true, the Build Sheet still matches what was actually built, and nothing on the deferred list crept in. One failure holds the gate; fix it before the watch week, because testing a build that drifted from its sheet proves nothing.
