You never flip a whole business onto a new system in one day. You earn your own trust first, then the team’s, then the customers’.
01
FIVE REAL CASES, YOUR THUMB ON THE BUTTON
Nothing automatic yet

Pick five real, current cases: customers you know well enough to phone if something lands wrong, covering your languages and typical order types. Run each through the system. The system drafts; you press every button. Note every wrong word, wrong figure, wrong timing in your “rules I learned” note, and have Claude fix each one the same day.

WHY FIVE, WHY THESE FIVE: five is enough to catch the wording, timing and language mistakes and small enough that you can personally repair any damage with one phone call. A system that works for five and grows will beat a complete system that nobody trusts.
02
THE WATCH WEEK
System beside the old way

For one full week the system drafts everything in its lane while your team keeps working the old way beside it. Each day, compare: what did the system prepare, and what did your people actually send or do? Every mismatch is either a fix for the system or a rule missing from your Build Sheet; update whichever is wrong the same day. The old way is not switched off; nothing is, yet.

DONE WHEN: a week passes in which the system’s drafts and reality agree, and every exception was caught by the “needs review” path before it went anywhere. If the week fails, fix and run another week. There is no shame in two watch weeks; there is real damage in zero.
03
THE SWITCH
Only what passed, only what is allowed

Turn on automatic behaviour only for what passed the watch week, and only what your blueprint’s “AI may act alone” list allows: reminders, status updates, internal notifications. Everything on the sign-off list stays human forever.

  • If sending to customers needs the business WhatsApp number, this is the week that clock (started in Builder Guide 01) pays off. If it has not finished verifying, keep drafting-for-approval until it has; never send customer messages from a personal number.
  • Widen in rings, never all at once: from five cases to one route, one department or one customer group. Hold each ring for a few days before the next.
  • One bad send: use the off switch, fix, re-test on five, switch on again. The off switch exists for exactly this moment; using it is the system working, not failing.
04
THE TEAM, AND THE REVIEW
Adoption is the finish line
  • One person first. Give the system to the one team member who feels the old pain most; their relief recruits the others. Never launch to the whole team by announcement.
  • The system hands people work; it does not watch them. Every screen answers “what should I do next”, not “who was slow”. Reports are a by-product of work done in the system, never a form anyone fills. This one design choice decides whether your team adopts the system or quietly kills it.
  • The old way retires only after the new way holds. Run both until the team member stops opening the old register on their own. Then the register goes in a drawer, not in the bin, for one more month.
  • The owner review runs off the diary. At the end of the switch week, read the log, not memories: what ran alone, what got flagged, what got corrected, and what each automatic action is costing. Adjust the guardrails, have Claude commit the adjustments, and lock them.
05
SYSTEM TWO
A new room in the same house

Back to Builder Guide 01 with the same blueprint: the deferred list on your First Build Card is the menu, and the data your first system has been writing is the head start. Same folder, same app, same database, same six rules: a new room in the same house. Each build is faster than the one before, because the house is already standing, the team already trusts the first room, and the data is already flowing.

THE STANDING TEST, EVERY MONTH: open the live app on your phone and ask three questions. Is the list still true? Did anything reach a customer without a yes it needed? Does the diary explain the week better than your memory does? Three yeses: build the next room. Any no: fix that before anything new.
